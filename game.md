# Project Codename: Chronicles of Iron and Legend

## Vision

Create a combat simulation RPG inspired by historical fighting manuals, ancient literature, mythology, folklore, religious texts, and medieval chronicles rather than modern tabletop RPGs.

The goal is to build a living world where every warrior, creature, beast, monster, spirit, and army follows the same underlying simulation rules.

A peasant with a spear, a knight in armor, a hawk in flight, a longbowman on a ridge, a minotaur in an arena, a giant from legend, and a dragon from myth all exist within the same combat engine.

The focus is:

1. Historical realism first.
2. Mythology second.
3. Fantasy third.

The game should feel less like a traditional fantasy RPG and more like a living world reconstructed from humanity’s oldest stories, wars, legends, and beliefs.

## 2. Ancient Sources

The world draws inspiration from:

- Greek mythology
- Roman mythology
- Norse mythology

---

## 3. Unified Simulation

Everything follows the same rules.

A knight:

- Has stamina
- Has morale
- Suffers wounds
- Uses reach and armor
- Can be exhausted, routed, captured, or killed

A giant:

- Has stamina
- Has morale
- Suffers wounds
- Uses reach and mass
- Can be exhausted, surrounded, wounded, routed, captured, or killed

A dragon:

- Has stamina
- Has morale
- Suffers wounds
- Uses flight, reach, terror, armor-like hide, and breath or natural weapons
- Can be exhausted, grounded, wounded, routed, captured, or killed

A hawk:

- Has stamina
- Has morale or instinctive behavior
- Uses flight
- Has limited durability
- Can scout, harass, flee, or attack small targets

No creature receives special treatment simply because it is magical or legendary.

Special traits should be modeled through the same simulation layers:

- Size
- Mass
- Reach
- Movement mode
- Armor or hide
- Wounds
- Stamina
- Morale
- Intelligence
- Instinct
- Training
- Equipment
- Supernatural influence, when appropriate

# Original Attribute and Trait Layer System

The game should move toward an original attribute model rather than copying modern fantasy RPG terminology.

The attribute system must support historical humans, animals, mythic creatures, spirits, monsters, civilians, commanders, formations, armies, adventuring parties, quest consequences, combat-earned experience, wounds, fear, morale, reputation, oaths, blessings, curses, and renown.

The goal is not merely to replace one set of ability names with another. The goal is to build a layered simulation where a character can begin as a peasant, become blooded in battle, gain scars, earn a name, take oaths, receive favor, suffer curses, and become part of a living chronicle.

---

## 13 Sheet Attributes

The game uses 13 main sheet attributes grouped into three layers:

1. **Body Layer**
2. **Mind and Field Layer**
3. **Order, Social, and Mythic Layer**

These attributes should be the clean project-safe vocabulary for new systems.

Avoid using proprietary or private tabletop terminology as the source of truth.

---

## Body Layer

| Attribute | Meaning | Main Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Might** | Raw bodily force, leverage, lifting power, and striking pressure | melee impact, grappling, shield pressure, carrying armor, breaking objects, resisting pushes |
| **Deftness** | Bodily coordination, weapon handling, balance, and precise control | strike accuracy, parry, dodge control, archery release, feints, weapon recovery, close-quarters control |
| **Vigor** | Bodily life-force, physical health, and wound tolerance | maximum health, shock resistance, serious injury survival, disease recovery, bodily resilience |
| **Endurance** | Stamina and ability to keep going under strain | fatigue, marching, fighting in armor, long combat, repeated actions, exhaustion resistance |
| **Mobility** | Effective movement across battlefield and terrain | pace, burst movement, pursuit, withdrawal, charge setup, flying, swimming, climbing, crawling, terrain crossing |

### Body Layer Notes

**Might** is not the same as body size, although size can modify Might-based outcomes.

**Deftness** is not total combat skill. It is body control. Training, technique, and weapon mastery belong in separate training layers.

**Vigor** and **Endurance** are separate because health and stamina are not identical. A person may be healthy but poorly conditioned, or exhausted but still physically hard to kill.

**Mobility** replaces the older idea of Speed. Mobility is not merely fast movement; it is the ability to move effectively in the conditions of battle.

---

## Mind and Field Layer

| Attribute | Meaning | Main Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Intellect** | Reasoning, memory, study, tactics, and learned understanding | planning, lore, recognizing weapons, reading maps, understanding formations, magical/religious study |
| **Awareness** | Perception, alertness, instinctive noticing, and battlefield reading | detecting ambushes, spotting openings, tracking targets, hearing threats, avoiding surprise |
| **Cunning** | Practical wit, deception, improvisation, and survival cleverness | traps, ambushes, tricks, feints, escape plans, dirty fighting, negotiation through guile |
| **Resolve** | Willpower, courage, pain tolerance, and mental endurance | fear resistance, morale stability, resisting panic, resisting intimidation, resisting curses or possession |

### Mind and Field Layer Notes

**Intellect** handles thinking and learning.

**Awareness** handles noticing.

**Cunning** handles practical cleverness under pressure.

**Resolve** handles not breaking.

These must remain separate. A scout can have high Awareness but low Intellect. A scholar can have high Intellect but poor battlefield Awareness. A veteran can have high Resolve but low Presence. A trickster can have high Cunning but low Discipline.

---

## Order, Social, and Mythic Layer

| Attribute | Meaning | Main Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Discipline** | Training, restraint, order, obedience to plan, and formation reliability | holding ranks, obeying commands, not chasing too early, reloading under pressure, marching in formation |
| **Presence** | Command force, intimidation, trust, social weight, and emotional influence | rallying allies, frightening enemies, forcing surrender, leadership, monster awe, priestly or royal authority |
| **Renown** | Name, reputation, honor, infamy, and deeds known by others | quest recognition, faction reactions, intimidation by reputation, titles, social consequences |
| **Favor** | Grace, blessing, goodwill, sacred standing, luck-like protection, or supernatural approval | blessings, holy protection, omens, social goodwill, temple standing, royal grace, mythic consequence |

### Order, Social, and Mythic Layer Notes

**Discipline** may begin from background and training, then improve through drill and battle. It is included as a sheet attribute because it matters in both duels and army warfare.

**Presence** affects others directly.

**Renown** is not charm. It is what the world has heard about the actor.

**Favor** is not generic luck. It represents goodwill, grace, blessing, sacred protection, or mythic approval depending on the setting context.

Renown and Favor should often begin low and grow through play.

# Attribute Design Rules

## Rule 1: Attributes Are Stable Foundations

Attributes should change slowly.

Combat should not constantly raise raw attributes. Instead, combat should usually grant traits, scars, training improvements, fear memories, confidence, renown, or wounds.

Examples:

- A soldier does not gain +5 Might after one battle.
- A soldier may gain **Blooded**, **Line Holder**, **Scarred Survivor**, or **Steady Under Arrows**.
- A commander may gain **Name Known at the Ford** or **Breaker of the South Gate**.
- A cowardly act may add **Known to Have Fled**.
- A sacred deed may add **Shrine-Favored**.

## Rule 2: Training Is Separate From Natural Ability

A high-Deftness actor is naturally coordinated.

A trained swordsman has sword training.

A high-Might actor is strong.

A trained wrestler knows how to use leverage.

Do not confuse attributes with training.

```js
training: {
  weapons: {
    spear: 12,
    shield: 10,
    bow: 6,
    dagger: 8
  },
  formationDrill: 11,
  fieldcraft: 8,
  healing: 3,
  horsemanship: 7
}
```

## Rule 3: States Are Not Attributes

Morale, fatigue, fear, wounds, and cohesion are changing states.

They should not be stored as fixed attributes.

```js
state: {
  morale: 75,
  fatigue: 20,
  fear: 5,
  wounds: [],
  cohesion: 80
}
```

## Rule 4: Traits Tell the Story

Traits record what happened.

A character sheet should gradually become a chronicle.

```js
traits: [
  "blooded",
  "line_holder",
  "scarred_survivor",
  "wolf_wound",
  "oath_fast"
]
```

# Trait Layer System

Traits are layered.

A trait should usually include:

- `id`
- `name`
- `layer`
- `rank`
- `source`
- `description`
- `effects`
- optional `drawbacks`
- optional `expires`
- optional `evidence` or `eventId`

## Trait Shape

```js
{
  id: "blooded",
  name: "Blooded",
  layer: "tempered",
  rank: 1,
  source: "combat",
  description: "Has survived real combat and is less likely to freeze during first shock.",
  effects: {
    resolveVsFear: 1,
    moraleShockReduction: 1
  }
}
```

---

## Trait Layers

| Layer | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural Traits** | Born, species-based, body-based, or innate traits | large frame, keen eyes, night sight, frail lungs, thick hide |
| **Background Traits** | Life before adventure or war | farm laborer, noble-born, temple-raised, border-born, fisher, hunter |
| **Training Traits** | Learned skills and drilled habits | spear drill, shield wall, longbow practice, mounted riding, herb lore |
| **Tempered Traits** | Gained through combat pressure | blooded, scarred survivor, rout survivor, steady under arrows |
| **Quest Traits** | Gained through adventure choices | oath-bound, shrine-favored, outlawed, king’s messenger, village defender |
| **Mythic Traits** | Gained through supernatural contact | cursed, blessed, spirit-marked, dragon-dread tested, relic-bearer |
| **Wound Traits** | Lasting injury consequences | lame leg, missing fingers, burn scar, old concussion, ruined eye |
| **Reputation Traits** | What the world says about the actor | giant-slayer, oathbreaker, merciful, cruel repute, coward, liberator |
| **Formation Traits** | Unit or army behavior traits | line holder, shield-wall drilled, pike trained, cavalry-broken |
| **Companion Traits** | Relationship and party-history traits | saved by ally, blood debt, sworn companion, rivalry, betrayed |
| **Cultural Traits** | Community, faction, or inherited social identity | border clan, temple household, forest folk, city watch, hill tribe |

# Combat-Earned Trait Examples

| Trigger | Trait | Possible Benefit | Possible Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survives first battle | **Blooded** | less first-shock fear | none |
| Holds formation while allies flee | **Line Holder** | formation morale bonus | may resist retreat orders |
| Survives being routed | **Rout Survivor** | better escape checks | fear when surrounded |
| Takes serious wound and lives | **Scarred Survivor** | Resolve bonus vs pain | visible scar or injury |
| Wins a duel | **Duel Proven** | Presence or Renown boost | challenged more often |
| Survives monster terror | **Monster-Dread Tested** | fear resistance vs monsters | nightmares before similar fights |
| Kills a giant creature | **Giant-Slayer** | Renown boost | enemies seek vengeance |
| Saves a wounded ally | **Shield of Companions** | ally loyalty boost | self-risking behavior |
| Breaks an oath | **Oathbreaker** | may gain Cunning reputation | social and sacred penalties |
| Keeps an oath under danger | **Oath-Fast** | Resolve/Favor/Renown boost | bound by public expectation |
| Refuses to kill surrendered enemy | **Merciful** | Favor/social trust | feared less by brutal enemies |
| Executes prisoners | **Dread Name** | intimidation | lowers trust and Favor |

# Renown System

Renown should not be one simple global number.

It should have local, regional, faction, and legendary scopes.

```js
renown: {
  local: 2,
  regional: 0,
  legendary: 0,
  faction: {
    north_watch: 4,
    temple_of_michael: 1,
    wolf_clans: -2
  },
  titles: ["Rout Survivor"],
  knownDeeds: ["Held the ford at dusk"]
}
```

## Renown Principles

- Local Renown grows from village, town, or battlefield events.
- Regional Renown grows from major deeds.
- Legendary Renown grows from mythic-scale events.
- Faction Renown may be positive or negative.
- A deed can increase Renown with one group and decrease it with another.
- Renown should modify dialogue, intimidation, recruitment, prices, allies, enemies, and morale.

---

# Favor System

Favor represents grace, blessing, goodwill, sacred protection, royal approval, or mythic approval.

Favor is context-based, not generic luck.

```js
favor: {
  sacred: 1,
  royal: 0,
  folk: 2,
  ancestral: 0,
  cursed: 0,
  faction: {
    temple_of_michael: 2,
    house_of_ash: -1
  }
}
```

## Favor Principles

- Favor can come from mercy, oath-keeping, service, sacrifice, piety, justice, hospitality, or heroic deeds.
- Favor can be lost through oath-breaking, betrayal, desecration, cruelty, cowardice, or cursed actions.
- Favor should affect mythic events, blessings, omens, social trust, and rare survival moments.
- Favor should not become a simple modern magic point system.

# Mobility System

Mobility replaces the older idea of Speed.

Mobility is not just how fast an actor moves. It represents effective movement across the battlefield and world.

A high-Mobility actor can close distance, withdraw, pursue, reposition, cross terrain, and exploit openings.

A low-Mobility actor may still be dangerous if strong, armored, disciplined, long-reaching, or terrifying.

## Mobility-Derived Values

| Derived Value | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Pace** | Normal controlled movement while armed and aware |
| **Burst** | Short sprint or sudden movement, usually costing fatigue |
| **Acceleration** | How quickly the actor begins moving or changes from stillness to motion |
| **Turn Control** | Ability to change direction without stumbling or losing guard |
| **Recovery Step** | Ability to reset after dodging, missing, being pushed, or overextending |
| **Charge Momentum** | Impact created by movement, mass, weapon reach, and Might |
| **Pursuit** | Ability to chase fleeing enemies or exploit a rout |
| **Withdrawal** | Ability to retreat without exposing the back or collapsing into panic |
| **Formation Pace** | Ability to keep movement rhythm with a unit |
| **Terrain Mobility** | Ability to move through mud, snow, hills, rubble, forest, water, stairs, or broken ground |

## Mobility Interactions

| Situation | Attributes and Traits Involved |
|---|---|
| Controlled retreat | Mobility + Deftness + Awareness + Resolve |
| Shield wall advance | Mobility + Discipline + formation training + commander Presence |
| Cavalry charge | Mobility + Might + mount mass + weapon reach + formation cohesion |
| Scout escape | Mobility + Awareness + Endurance |
| Heavy knight closing distance | Mobility + Endurance + armor burden |
| Giant strike recovery | Deftness + Mobility + size/mass |
| Panic flight | Mobility + failed Resolve |
| Pursuit after rout | Mobility + Endurance + Awareness |

# Legacy Attribute Migration

Compatibility actors may still contain older or public-baseline ability names.

The adapter should translate them into the original attribute system when possible.

| Compatibility Field | Original Field |
|---|---|
| strength | might |
| dexterity | deftness and/or mobility depending on context |
| constitution | vigor and/or endurance depending on context |
| intelligence | intellect |
| wisdom | awareness and/or resolve depending on context |
| charisma | presence, renown, or bearing depending on context |
| movement | mobility-derived pace |
| speed-like values | mobility-derived pace, burst, or movement mode |
| hitPoints / hp | health derived from vigor and conditions |
| armorClass | armor / defense compatibility bridge |

Compatibility fields may remain during migration, but new systems should prefer the original names.

# Implementation Priorities

Add the new fields without breaking existing combat.

- Add `attributes` with the 13 original names.
- Add `training`.
- Add `traits`.
- Add `state`.
- Add `reputation`.
- Add `favor`.
- Add `behavior`.
- Add `movement` with modes and derived values.

Create a normalization adapter that maps old actors into the new shape.

Do not delete legacy actors that currently work.

The core unit of the game is the actor.

An actor can be:

- An animal
- A mythic creature
- A spirit
- A monster
- A soldier
- A civilian
- A commander
- A passive noncombatant
- A defensive guard
- A player-created character
- A generated enemy
- A normalized legacy creature
- A member of an army

Playable does not mean party-side.

An actor may be:

- Player-controlled
- AI-controlled
- Autoplay-controlled
- Passive
- Defensive
- Neutral
- Hostile
- Allied
- Part of a custom faction
- Part of a formation or army

Separate these concepts:

- `team`, `side`, `battleSide`, `armyId`, or `factionId` determines allegiance.
- `controlMode` determines who controls the actor.
- `playable` determines whether the actor can be manually selected or controlled.
- `type` is legacy metadata and should not be the source of truth for allegiance.

Examples:

```js
{
  name: "Minotaur",
  team: "enemy",
  controlMode: "ai",
  playable: true,
  modelKey: "minotaur"
}
```

```js
{
  name: "Longbowman",
  team: "party",
  controlMode: "manual",
  playable: true,
  modelKey: "longbowman"
}
```

```js
{
  name: "Hawk",
  team: "enemy",
  controlMode: "ai",
  playable: true,
  movementModes: ["ground", "flying"],
  modelKey: "hawk"
}
```

Do not assume:

- Enemies are always AI-only.
- Party members are always manually controlled.
- `type: "player"` means party-side.
- `type: "enemy"` means AI-only.
- A `playable-*` id means party-side.
- An enemy cannot be manually controlled.
- The player can only control party members.

# Control Mode Philosophy

Control mode is first-class.

The simulator should support:

- Player versus AI
- AI versus AI
- Player-controlled enemies
- AI-controlled party members
- Mixed armies
- Passive actors
- Defensive actors
- Neutral factions
- Large battle autoplay
- Future campaign simulation

Allowed control modes should include:

- `manual`
- `ai`
- `autoplay`
- `passive`
- `defensive`

Expected defaults:

- Party + manual mode = human controlled
- Party + AI mode = AI controlled ally
- Enemy + AI mode = AI controlled opponent
- Enemy + manual mode = player-controlled enemy
- Neutral civilian, merchant, or dialogue actor = passive
- Guard or defender = defensive until provoked or scripted

A side with no manual actor should not stall.

AI mode should eventually allow the user to press a toggle and watch a fight play out.

AI autoplay should support:

- Party versus enemies
- Enemies versus enemies
- Custom faction battles
- Player-controlled enemies mixed with AI allies
- Large battle tests

# Movement Philosophy

Movement must support multiple movement modes.

Do not collapse movement into a single ground speed.

Preserve or normalize:

- Ground movement
- Flying movement
- Climbing
- Swimming
- Crawling
- Mounted movement
- Dash/burst distance
- Compatibility movement-rate attributes

A flying actor should have clear movement fields, such as:

```js
movementModes: ["ground", "flying"],
movement: {
  ground: 10,
  flying: 60
}
```

or the closest existing project-compatible equivalent.

Flight mechanics for hawks and other flying characters must be preserved.

A flying actor should not be treated as a ground-only combatant unless the actor is grounded, wounded, trapped, exhausted, or otherwise restricted by the simulation.

## Mobility Naming Note

For new systems, do not use Speed as the name of the core attribute. Use **Mobility** for the attribute and use **Pace**, **Burst**, **Pursuit**, **Withdrawal**, **Recovery Step**, and **Terrain Mobility** as derived movement concepts. Existing speed-like compatibility fields may remain during migration.

# Ranged Combat Philosophy

Ranged actors must be able to fight at range.

Longbowman, Archer, Crossbowman, and other ranged actors should not be forced to close into melee when they have a valid ranged attack.

A normalized ranged actor should include:

```js
{
  aiRole: "ranged",
  attacks: [
    {
      name: "Longbow Shot",
      kind: "ranged",
      rangeProfile: {
        normal: 150,
        long: 600
      }
    },
    {
      name: "Knife Attack",
      kind: "melee",
      reach: 5
    }
  ]
}
```

Use project-safe and original naming.

Do not copy private or proprietary rulebook terminology.

If a ranged actor is 80 ft away and has a valid ranged attack with normal range greater than 80 ft, the AI should prefer shooting rather than repeatedly attempting movement.

Ranged combat should eventually consider:

- Line of sight
- Cover
- Elevation
- Wind
- Ammunition
- Reload time
- Weapon draw
- Fatigue
- Range bands
- Target movement
- Formation density
- Friendly fire risk
- Morale impact

# Legacy / Compatibility Migration Philosophy

Do not delete old working features just because their data shape is messy.

Migrate and normalize them.

Examples:

- Longbowman should keep its model and arrow-firing behavior.
- Hawk and flying creatures should keep flight mechanics.
- Minotaur should exist as its own mythic brute archetype, not collapse into Arena Champion.
- Arena Champion should remain a separate arena/training opponent.
- Legacy creatures, soldiers, and enemies may become normalized selectable actors.
- Compatibility fields may remain as bridges, but should not be the primary source of truth for new systems.

When a legacy actor behaves strangely, do not immediately remove it.

First ask:

1. What feature was this actor supposed to prove?
2. Which parts still work?
3. Which fields are legacy or unsafe?
4. What normalized actor shape should replace the raw legacy body?
5. How can the model, attacks, movement, and AI role be preserved?

Legacy or compatibility actors may remain available, but must be clearly labeled.

Use labels such as:

- `source: "compatibility-actor"`
- `sourceLabel: "Compatibility Actor"`
- `source: "normalized-legacy-actor"`
- `sourceLabel: "Normalized Legacy Actor"`

Compatibility actors must pass through an adapter before entering combat.

The adapter should normalize:

- team
- controlMode
- playable
- modelKey
- movement modes
- ability scores
- compatibility attributes
- HP
- armor
- attacks
- range
- action count
- AI role

Do not allow old compatibility-only movement or range fields to override clean original/public fields unless explicitly intended.

# Armor and Equipment Philosophy

Armor should be physical and layered.

Armor is not just a single defense number.

Armor should eventually consider:

- Clothing or base layer
- Padding
- Mail
- Plate
- Helmet
- Gloves
- Arm protection
- Leg protection
- Foot protection
- Shield
- Outer garments
- Horse armor
- Natural hide
- Scales
- Bone
- Supernatural protection

Weapons should matter by form and context.

Examples:

- A longbow is useful at range but unsuitable inside a grapple.
- A dagger is weak in open melee but useful in close quarters.
- A spear controls distance.
- A pike controls formation space.
- A sword is versatile.
- A shield protects but also shapes movement.
- A lance depends on mounted speed and formation context.
- Claws and horns behave differently from metal weapons.

# Grapple and Close-Quarters Philosophy

Grapple is a tactical close-quarters option, not just another attack.

Design intent:

- Grapple is strong against armored fighters because it closes inside their normal weapon range.
- Grapple can create chances to attack weak points in armor with a dagger, knife, short blade, or natural weapon.
- Grapple should cost stamina.
- Grapple should create risk of reversal if the grappler becomes exhausted.
- Non-grapplers, archers, casters, light fighters, and reach-weapon users should usually avoid grapple.
- Reach weapons should matter by keeping enemies one or more hexes away.

Inside a grapple:

- Long weapons should become awkward or unusable.
- Short weapons become more useful.
- Armor can still protect.
- Weak-point attacks should depend on control, advantage, high roll, fatigue, or critical events.
- Ground fighting should be dangerous and exhausting.

Do not create a second grapple system if one already exists.

Reuse and improve the existing grapple state where possible.
