# Project Codename: Chronicles of Iron and Legend

## Vision

Create a combat simulation RPG inspired by historical fighting manuals, ancient literature, mythology, folklore, religious texts, and medieval chronicles rather than modern tabletop RPGs.

The goal is to build a living world where every warrior, creature, and army follows the same underlying simulation rules.

A peasant with a spear, a knight in armor, a giant from legend, and a dragon from myth all exist within the same combat engine.

The focus is realism first, mythology second, and fantasy third.

---

# Core Principles

## 1. Historical Foundation

Combat should be rooted in real historical fighting methods whenever possible.

Sources may include:

- Medieval fight books
- Historical military manuals
- Chronicles and battle records
- Ancient warfare texts
- Archaeological evidence

Examples:

- Sword and buckler fighting
- Longsword fencing
- Spear combat
- Shield wall tactics
- Mounted combat
- Polearms
- Archery
- Grappling

Combat should emphasize:

- Positioning
- Reach
- Timing
- Stamina
- Morale
- Wounds
- Armor protection

---

## 2. Ancient Sources

The world draws inspiration from:

- Greek mythology
- Roman mythology
- Norse mythology
- Celtic folklore
- Germanic legends
- Anglo-Saxon literature
- Biblical traditions
- Medieval bestiaries
- Epic poems
- Ancient chronicles

The game should avoid dependence on modern fantasy tropes whenever possible.

---

## 3. Unified Simulation

Everything follows the same rules.

Examples:

A knight:

- Has stamina
- Has morale
- Suffers wounds

A giant:

- Has stamina
- Has morale
- Suffers wounds

A dragon:

- Has stamina
- Has morale
- Suffers wounds

No creature receives special treatment simply because it is magical.

---

# Tier Structure

## Tier 1: Historical

Pure historical simulation.

No monsters.

No supernatural powers.

Examples:

- Knight
- Man-at-arms
- Spearman
- Archer
- Longbowman
- Crossbowman
- Raider
- Mercenary
- Horseman
- Scout

Goal:

Answer questions like:

"What happens when 20 spearmen fight 10 armored knights?"

---

## Tier 2: Mythic

Historical world with legendary creatures.

Examples:

- Minotaur
- Cyclops
- Harpy
- Giant
- Dragon
- Sea Serpent
- Chimera
- Sphinx
- Troll
- Hidden Folk

Goal:

Explore how real soldiers would respond to legendary threats.

---

## Tier 3: Legendary

Adds supernatural forces.

Examples:

- Blessings
- Curses
- Prophecy
- Divination
- Spirit influence
- Miracles
- Transformation
- Rune magic
- Relics

Magic should feel ancient and mysterious rather than modern spell-casting.

---

## Tier 4: Warfare

Large-scale battle simulation.

Players command:

- Squads
- Companies
- Warbands
- Armies

Historical formations include:

- Shield Wall
- Phalanx
- Wedge
- Testudo
- Square
- Skirmish Line
- Cavalry Charge

The battle system should allow zooming between:

- Army Level
- Formation Level
- Individual Soldier Level

A player should be able to watch a battle of thousands while still examining a single warrior.

---

# Combat Engine

Every combatant tracks:

- Health
- Stamina
- Morale
- Position
- Reach
- Armor
- Wounds
- Fatigue

Potential wound locations:

- Head
- Face
- Neck
- Torso
- Arms
- Hands
- Legs
- Feet

Combat outcomes:

- Death
- Unconsciousness
- Surrender
- Rout
- Capture
- Retreat

---

# Warfare Engine

Every formation tracks:

- Morale
- Fatigue
- Cohesion
- Supply
- Ammunition
- Commander quality

Armies can collapse from morale before complete destruction.

Historical outcomes:

- Victory
- Defeat
- Rout
- Withdrawal
- Encirclement
- Pursuit

---

# Long-Term Goal

Create a living historical-mythic simulation where:

- Every fighter matters.
- Every creature follows consistent rules.
- Armies emerge from individual warriors.
- Mythology grows naturally from historical foundations.
- Ancient stories become interactive simulations.

The game should feel less like a traditional fantasy RPG and more like a living world reconstructed from humanity's oldest stories, wars, legends, and beliefs.
